# Business Investor Validator — Worked Examples

## Example A: Strong Idea (Green Light)

**User input:** "I want to build a B2B SaaS platform that helps e-commerce brands automate
influencer payments and contracts. There are thousands of small brands using spreadsheets."

---

### 🎯 Idea Summary
A B2B SaaS platform automating influencer payment workflows and contract management for
e-commerce brands. Targets a fragmented, underserved segment currently relying on manual processes.

### 📊 Scorecard

| Dimension                        | Score  | Rationale |
|----------------------------------|--------|-----------|
| Market Size & TAM                | 8/10   | Influencer marketing is a $21B+ industry growing 15%/year; e-commerce brands are a large, reachable segment |
| Revenue Model & Unit Economics   | 8/10   | SaaS subscription model; clear LTV potential with sticky workflows; low marginal cost at scale |
| Scalability & Defensibility      | 7/10   | Payments data creates switching costs over time; network effects possible if influencers onboard too |
| Competitive Landscape            | 7/10   | Grin and Aspire exist but target enterprise; clear wedge for SMB/mid-market brands |
| **TOTAL (weighted)**             | **75/100** | |

### ✅ Verdict
🟢 **Green Light** — Solid fundamentals with a clear wedge. The SMB focus in a growing market
with spreadsheet-based incumbency is a classic SaaS opportunity. Execution risk is the main
variable.

### 💪 Strengths
- Strong product-market fit signal: spreadsheet users are the ideal SaaS prospect
- Sticky product category — payment workflows are hard to leave once integrated
- Growing market with regulatory tailwinds (1099s, compliance requirements)

### ⚠️ Key Risks & Gaps
- Grin/Aspire could move downmarket; pricing pressure risk
- Payment infrastructure is complex — likely needs Stripe Connect or similar
- CAC could be high if brands are hard to reach via paid channels

### 🔧 How to Strengthen It
- **Revenue Model**: Add a transaction fee (0.5–1%) on top of subscriptions — aligns pricing
  with value delivered and adds a high-margin revenue stream.
- **Competitive Landscape**: Define the exact brand segment (e.g., Shopify stores $1M–$10M
  revenue) to sharpen go-to-market and avoid head-on competition.

### 💡 Clarifying Questions
1. Do you have any early users or design partners? Even 3–5 validate the pain.
2. Are you planning to handle payments in-app or just the contracting/tracking layer?
3. What's your go-to-market motion — content, outbound, partnerships with Shopify apps?

## Example B: Weak Idea (Red Flag)

**User input:** "I want to build an app where people can rate restaurants and leave reviews,
with a social feed. Like Yelp but more social."

---

### 🎯 Idea Summary
A restaurant review and discovery app with a social feed layer. Positioned as a more social,
community-driven alternative to Yelp.

### 📊 Scorecard

| Dimension                        | Score  | Rationale |
|----------------------------------|--------|-----------|
| Market Size & TAM                | 6/10   | Restaurant discovery is large, but the market is dominated; "social" layer doesn't expand TAM |
| Revenue Model & Unit Economics   | 3/10   | Unclear — ads? Premium listings? Both require massive scale and face Yelp/Google competition |
| Scalability & Defensibility      | 3/10   | Classic cold-start problem: reviews are worthless without users; no moat until then |
| Competitive Landscape            | 2/10   | Yelp, Google Maps, TripAdvisor, OpenTable, Instagram already own this space |
| **TOTAL (weighted)**             | **35/100** | |

### ✅ Verdict
🔴 **Red Flag** — The market is dominated by entrenched players with massive network effects
and brand recognition. "More social" is not a strong enough wedge to unseat any of them.

### 💪 Strengths
- Restaurant discovery is a real, recurring consumer need
- Social features could create engagement if executed well

### ⚠️ Key Risks & Gaps
- No differentiated wedge — "social" is not enough against Google Maps
- Cold-start problem is severe: reviews are only valuable with volume
- Monetization depends on scale that's nearly impossible to reach without 9-figure capital

### 🔧 How to Strengthen It
- **Competitive Landscape**: Niche down radically — e.g., "restaurant discovery for vegans"
  or "hidden gems in a specific city" — to find a defensible wedge before expanding.
- **Revenue Model**: Consider a B2B model (sell insights to restaurants, not ads to consumers)
  to generate revenue without needing massive consumer scale.
- **Scalability**: Explore UGC virality loops — what makes users share reviews, not just write them?

### 💡 Clarifying Questions
1. What's the specific reason Yelp/Google Maps fail users today — what's your origin story?
2. Is there a specific city or community where you'd launch first to solve the cold-start problem?
3. Have you talked to 20+ potential users about their actual pain with existing review apps?
